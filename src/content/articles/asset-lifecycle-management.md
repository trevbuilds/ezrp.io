  Asset lifecycle management is the acquire-to-retire path for physical assets: planned, acquired, commissioned, operated, renewed and disposed of, with the cost of each stage recorded against the asset.
  Done properly it is what makes a renewal programme defensible.
---

## Why it matters

Assets are usually managed well in operation and badly at the two ends. Commissioning data never makes it into the register, and disposal happens physically long before it happens in the ledger.

- **Whole-of-life cost is the decision basis**: Purchase price is the smallest part of what an asset costs.
- **The ends are where data is lost**: Handover from a capital project and disposal both leak.
- **Renewal funding depends on it**: A renewal case built on age alone will not survive scrutiny; one built on condition and cost will.

## The workflow

```steps
Planning :: Need established against service level, condition and risk, not replacement age.
Acquisition :: Procured with the data the register will need specified in the contract.
Commissioning :: Asset created, capitalised, handed over with documentation and maintenance regime attached.
Operation and maintenance :: Cost, condition and failure history accumulated against the asset.
Condition assessment :: Assessed on a cycle, so renewal decisions rest on evidence.
Renewal or disposal :: Decided on whole-of-life cost; disposal recorded financially and physically together.
```

> Specify the asset data in the procurement contract. Chasing serial numbers, warranty terms and maintenance schedules from a contractor after handover costs more than asking for them as a deliverable.

## What you need in place

- **Capital project to asset register handover**: A defined deliverable, with the data specified.
- **Condition assessment on a cycle**: With a consistent scale across asset classes.
- **Whole-of-life cost capture**: Acquisition, operation, maintenance and disposal, against the asset.
- **A disposal process that closes both records**: Physical removal and financial derecognition.
- **Componentisation where it matters**: Assets with parts on different lives depreciate wrongly as one.

## Questions to ask

```qa
Handover :: What data arrives with an asset from a capital project? :: Usually less than the register needs.
Ghost assets :: Are we depreciating assets that no longer exist? :: Verification against the physical almost always finds some.
Condition :: When was each critical asset last assessed? :: Renewal cases built on age are weaker than they look.
Whole-of-life :: Can we state the total cost of owning a given asset? :: If not, replace-or-maintain is being decided on intuition.
Componentisation :: Are long-life and short-life parts separated? :: A pump and its building on one asset record depreciate wrongly.
```

## Metrics and KPIs to track

```metrics
Register accuracy :: Assets verified against physical existence.
Condition assessment currency :: Critical assets assessed within their cycle.
Whole-of-life cost :: Per asset class, against replacement value.
Capitalisation lag :: Time from commissioning to the asset appearing in the register.
Disposal lag :: Time between physical disposal and financial derecognition.
```
