## Why it matters

Escalation is not free. Every decision that travels up and back is days of latency, and the person who eventually decides usually knows less about the specifics than the person who raised it.

- **Latency compounds**: Ten decisions a week each waiting five days is a workstream operating at half speed.
- **Quality falls with distance**: Decisions made away from the detail get made on the summary.
- **Ownership follows authority**: People who did not decide do not own the outcome.

## Putting it into practice

```steps
Map the decisions :: What actually needs deciding, and how often.
Set thresholds :: What can be settled in the workstream, and what genuinely must escalate.
Delegate explicitly :: Written, so nobody has to guess whether they can.
Give the context :: Delegation without the constraints produces decisions that have to be undone.
Support rather than review :: Available to consult, not required to approve.
Hold the outcome :: Accountability sits with the decision-maker, including when it goes wrong.
```

> The test of delegation is what happens to a reasonable decision that turns out badly. If it is reversed and the authority quietly withdrawn, nobody will use the delegation again.

## What you need in place

- **A decision map with thresholds**: Financial, scope and design, written down.
- **Business representatives who hold delegation**: One who must check is a message-carrier.
- **Context and constraints shared**: So delegated decisions stay inside the boundaries.
- **A consult route, not an approval route**: For the genuinely uncertain.
- **Tolerance for reasonable failure**: Or delegation is theoretical.

## Questions to ask

```qa
Thresholds :: What can a workstream lead decide alone? :: If undefined, everything escalates by default.
Representation :: Can our business representatives commit? :: If not, we have consultation, not decision-making.
Latency :: How long does a typical design decision take? :: Measure it; the number is usually a surprise.
Reversal :: How often are delegated decisions overturned? :: Frequent reversal withdraws the delegation in practice.
Context :: Do decision-makers know the constraints? :: Delegation without context produces expensive corrections.
```

## Metrics and KPIs to track

```metrics
Decision latency :: By decision type and by level.
Escalation rate :: Decisions escalated against those decided locally.
Reversal rate :: Delegated decisions later overturned.
Decision backlog :: Open decisions awaiting resolution, and their age.
```
