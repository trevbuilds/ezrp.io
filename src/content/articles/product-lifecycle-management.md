## Why it matters

The handover from design to production is where most product cost is locked in and most version confusion begins. A change approved in engineering that never reaches the shop floor is a defect that will be found by a customer.

- **Design decisions determine cost**: Most of a product's cost is committed before anything is made.
- **Version control is a safety matter**: Building to a superseded revision is a recall waiting to happen.
- **Compliance evidence lives here**: Certification and regulatory approval attach to a specific revision.

## The workflow

```steps
Concept development :: Need, market and feasibility assessed before design effort is committed.
Design engineering :: Product designed, with the bill of materials and specifications built as the design artefact.
Prototype testing :: Validated against requirement, with results recorded against the revision.
Product launch :: Released to production with the BOM, routing, costing and documentation complete.
```

> Engineering change is the whole problem. Decide how a change reaches production, what happens to stock built to the old revision, and who approves the cut-in point — before the first change, not during it.

## What you need in place

- **Revision control on the bill of materials**: With an effective date and a cut-in rule.
- **An engineering change process**: Assessed for stock, cost, tooling and compliance impact.
- **A single source for specifications**: Engineering and production reading the same document.
- **Costing at design**: So the margin is known before commitment, not after launch.
- **Compliance records by revision**: Certification is not transferable between revisions.
- **End-of-life planning**: Run-out of stock, spares obligation and service commitment.

## Questions to ask

```qa
Revision :: Does production build to the current approved revision? :: Verify rather than assume; the gap is common.
Change :: How does an engineering change reach the shop floor? :: The answer is often email.
Stock impact :: What happens to stock built to the old revision? :: Decide before the change, not after.
Costing :: Is product cost known at design? :: Costing at launch is costing too late to act.
Compliance :: Which revision is certified? :: Certification does not follow the product automatically.
End of life :: What is our spares obligation after discontinuation? :: It is usually contractual and usually forgotten.
```

## Metrics and KPIs to track

```metrics
Time to market :: Concept to launch, by product.
Engineering change volume :: Post-launch changes, which indicate design maturity.
Change implementation lag :: Approval to production effectivity.
Cost at launch against target :: Design-stage estimate to actual.
Revision compliance :: Production orders built to the current revision.
```
