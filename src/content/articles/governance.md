## Why it matters

Governance is often built as a reporting structure and then used as a decision structure, which is why steering committees receive status packs and produce no decisions. The design question is not who attends — it is what each forum can settle.

- **It converts escalation into decision**: A defined path means an issue ends somewhere rather than circulating.
- **It protects the schedule**: Decision latency is a direct schedule cost and it compounds.
- **It creates the audit trail**: For public and government-owned entities, evidence of how decisions were made is itself a requirement.

## The workflow

```steps
Structure design :: Forums defined by what they decide, not by seniority.
Terms of reference :: Each forum's authority, membership, quorum, cadence and inputs, written down.
Delegation mapping :: Financial and scope authority traced back to the organisation's delegation instrument.
Operation :: Forums run to cadence with papers circulated ahead, not read in the room.
Decision recording :: Every decision captured with its rationale, owner and date.
Escalation :: A defined route and a service level for issues that exceed a forum's authority.
Review :: Governance itself reviewed — forums that decide nothing get removed.
```

> Design each forum around the decisions it owns. A forum with no decision rights will fill its time with status, and the decisions it should have made will be taken by whoever is closest to the deadline.

## What you need in place

- **Terms of reference per forum**: Including what it explicitly cannot decide.
- **Delegation alignment**: Programme approval limits consistent with the organisation's own instrument.
- **A decision log**: One, owned, and referenced rather than rewritten.
- **Papers ahead of time**: With a recommendation, not just information.
- **Escalation service levels**: How long an issue may sit before it must move up.
- **Business representation with authority**: A representative who must check with someone else is a delay, not a decision-maker.

## Questions to ask

```qa
Decision rights :: What does each forum actually decide? :: If the answer is unclear, it is a reporting meeting.
Authority :: Do the attendees hold the delegation they are exercising? :: Approvals outside delegation are a finding waiting to be made.
Latency :: How long does a typical decision take? :: Measure it. It is usually the largest single source of slip.
Reversal :: How often are decisions reopened? :: Repeated reopening means the wrong people are deciding.
Evidence :: Would an auditor find the rationale for a material decision? :: Minutes recording the outcome without the reasoning fail this.
```

## Metrics and KPIs to track

```metrics
Decision latency :: Days from request to decision, by forum.
Decisions reopened :: Share of decisions revisited after being made.
Escalation ageing :: Issues sitting beyond their escalation service level.
Forum attendance :: Quorum achieved, and delegate attendance instead of members.
Papers on time :: Circulated ahead of the meeting as required.
```
