## Why it matters

Mainstream products in the same tier are more alike than their sales cycles suggest. What differs materially is fit to your particular constraints, the partner doing the work, and the total cost across ten years rather than three.

- **The partner is the larger variable**: The same product succeeds and fails depending on who implements it and who from your side works with them.
- **Requirements must be weighted before demonstrations**: After a demonstration, criteria drift towards what was shown well.
- **Total cost is ten years**: Licence or subscription, implementation, internal effort, support, and the next upgrade.

## The workflow

```steps
Requirements :: Documented and weighted — must, should, could — before any vendor sees them.
Market scan :: A shortlist appropriate to the organisation's size and complexity, not to its aspiration.
Scripted demonstrations :: Vendors demonstrate your scenarios with your data, not their showcase.
Reference checks :: Organisations of similar size and sector, contacted directly, asked what went wrong.
Partner assessment :: The named individuals proposed, their availability, and their record on comparable work.
Commercial evaluation :: Ten-year total cost, including the internal share and the exit position.
Decision and contract :: Scored against the weighted criteria, with the recorded rationale.
```

> Assess the people, not the firm. Contract the named individuals with named availability, because the team at the pitch is very often not the team that arrives.

## What you need in place

- **Weighted criteria agreed before contact with vendors**: And not changed after a demonstration.
- **Your own scripted scenarios**: Including the awkward ones and your real volumes.
- **Independent references**: Ones you found, not only ones supplied.
- **Ten-year cost model**: With the internal effort included.
- **Exit terms**: Data extraction rights and format, agreed at signature not at divorce.
- **Probity discipline where it applies**: Public and government-owned entities have procurement obligations that govern the whole process.

## Questions to ask

```qa
Weighting :: Were criteria weighted before demonstrations? :: If after, the process has been shaped by the demonstrations.
Scenarios :: Did vendors demonstrate our scenarios or theirs? :: Only one of those tells you anything.
Team :: Who are the named consultants, and are they contracted? :: The pitch team's presence is not a commitment.
References :: Did we find references ourselves? :: Supplied references are selected, not representative.
Total cost :: What is the ten-year cost including our own effort? :: Three-year comparisons favour whoever defers cost.
Exit :: How do we get our data out, in what format? :: Agreed now, or it is a negotiation under pressure.
Fit :: Which requirements does this not meet, and what will we do instead? :: Every product fails some; know which before signing.
```

## Metrics and KPIs to track

```metrics
Requirement fit :: Weighted score by product, standard against configured against built.
Reference coverage :: Independent references contacted.
Total cost of ownership :: Ten-year, by component, by option.
Key personnel commitment :: Named consultants contracted with availability.
Decision traceability :: Criteria, scores and rationale recorded.
```
