## Why it matters

In an asset-intensive organisation the asset register is not a finance artefact; it is the operating model. The decision to maintain, refurbish or replace is made against condition and criticality, and it is made every day.

- **It holds most of the spend**: Maintenance labour, materials and contractors dominate operating cost.
- **It carries safety and regulatory obligation**: Inspection regimes for pressure, electrical, lifting and water assets are legal requirements with evidence attached.
- **It is where finance and operations disagree**: The financial register and the physical register describe the same assets differently, and reconciling them is real work.

## The workflow

```steps
Asset tracking :: The register maintained against what physically exists, with location, criticality and condition.
Preventive maintenance scheduling :: Work planned from time, usage or condition, against the regime each asset requires.
Work order management :: Work raised, scheduled, resourced, executed and closed with actuals captured.
Asset performance monitoring :: Failures, downtime and cost tracked against the asset, not just the cost centre.
Replacement planning :: Renewal decided on condition, criticality and whole-of-life cost rather than age alone.
```

> The financial asset register and the maintenance asset register are almost never the same list. Decide deliberately which is the master for which attribute, and reconcile on a cycle — otherwise depreciation runs on assets that were scrapped two years ago.

## What you need in place

- **A hierarchy that matches how work is done**: Functional location and asset, so history stays with the location when the asset is swapped.
- **Criticality assessment**: What fails, how badly, and how quickly it must be restored.
- **Maintenance strategy per asset class**: Run-to-failure is a legitimate strategy when chosen, not when defaulted to.
- **Work order actuals**: Labour, materials and downtime captured, or cost per asset is fiction.
- **A link to finance**: Capitalisation of renewal work, and the depreciation consequence.
- **Mobile capability**: Technicians record in the field or they record later, which means approximately.

## Questions to ask

```qa
Registers :: Do the financial and maintenance registers agree? :: They usually do not, and the difference is worth quantifying before a replacement.
Criticality :: Which assets would stop the service if they failed? :: This drives maintenance strategy and spares holding.
Actuals :: Do work orders capture real labour and materials? :: Without it, whole-of-life cost cannot be calculated.
Planned ratio :: What share of maintenance is planned rather than reactive? :: It is the single clearest measure of maturity.
Compliance :: Which inspections are legally mandated, and where is the evidence? :: Regulators ask for the record, not the intention.
Capitalisation :: When does maintenance become renewal? :: The boundary should be a written rule, not a judgement call per job.
```

## Metrics and KPIs to track

```metrics
Planned maintenance ratio :: Planned work as a share of total maintenance hours.
Schedule compliance :: Planned work completed in its window.
Mean time between failures :: By asset class and criticality.
Asset availability :: Uptime for critical assets against target.
Maintenance cost per asset :: Against replacement value, trended.
Register reconciliation :: Variance between financial and maintenance registers.
Compliance inspection currency :: Mandated inspections completed on time.
```
