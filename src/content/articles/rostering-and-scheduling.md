## Why it matters

A roster is a cost commitment. Penalty rates, overtime and allowances are largely determined at the point the roster is published, not at the point pay is calculated.

- **It sets cost in advance**: A shift pattern that crosses a penalty boundary costs more, and the roster is where that choice is made.
- **It carries obligations**: Minimum breaks, maximum consecutive shifts, notice of change and fatigue rules are roster constraints, not payroll ones.
- **It affects people directly**: Roster stability is one of the strongest drivers of retention in shift workforces.

## The workflow

```steps
Demand forecast :: Required coverage is estimated by time, place and skill.
Availability and constraints :: Employee availability, qualifications, fatigue rules and instrument limits are gathered.
Roster construction :: Shifts are assigned against demand, cost and constraint.
Publication :: The roster is issued with the notice the instrument requires.
Variation :: Changes are handled with the right notice and approval, and recorded.
Actual against roster :: Worked time is compared with the roster, and the variance explained.
```

> The variance between rostered and actual is where cost leaks. If nobody reviews it weekly, the roster stops being a control and becomes a suggestion.

## What you need in place

- **Skills and qualifications**: Held as data, including expiry — an expired ticket should block an assignment.
- **Fatigue and instrument rules**: Encoded as constraints, not left to the roster writer's memory.
- **Cost visibility at build time**: The roster writer sees the cost of a pattern before publishing it.
- **Notice rules**: Publication and change notice per the instrument.
- **Integration with time capture**: So actual against rostered is measurable rather than anecdotal.

## Questions to ask

```qa
Cost visibility :: Does the person building the roster see what it will cost? :: If not, cost control happens after the money is committed.
Constraints :: Are fatigue and break rules enforced by the system or by the roster writer? :: Memory is not a control.
Qualifications :: Can someone be rostered to a task they are not qualified for? :: In safety-critical work this is the first question an investigator asks.
Notice :: What notice does the instrument require, and does the system enforce it? :: Short-notice changes carry penalties in some awards.
Variance :: Who reviews rostered against actual, and how often? :: Weekly, or the roster is decorative.
```

## Metrics and KPIs to track

```metrics
Rostered against actual hours :: Variance by area, with reasons.
Overtime share :: Hours paid at overtime rates as a share of total.
Roster change rate :: Changes after publication, and how many were short notice.
Unfilled shifts :: Coverage gaps, and how they were resolved.
Qualification breaches :: Assignments blocked or overridden.
```
