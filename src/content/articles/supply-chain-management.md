## Why it matters

Supply chain decisions become financial outcomes with a delay. Stock bought today is working capital now and obsolescence later; a supplier chosen on price is a service failure two quarters out.

- **It ties up working capital**: Inventory is cash that has already been spent and not yet earned.
- **It determines the customer promise**: Availability is what makes a delivery date real.
- **It carries concentration risk**: Single-source dependency is invisible until it is not.

## The workflow

```steps
Supplier management :: Suppliers qualified, contracted and measured against what was agreed.
Purchase ordering :: Demand converted to commitment, priced from contract rather than memory.
Goods receiving :: What arrived recorded accurately and promptly — the evidence payables depends on.
Inventory control :: Stock counted, valued, located and aged, with accuracy verified rather than assumed.
Order fulfilment :: Customer demand met from available stock or capacity, allocated by policy.
Logistics management :: Movement planned, executed and tracked through to proof of delivery.
```

> Stock accuracy is the constraint on everything else. Planning, promising and fulfilment all consume the same number, and if it is wrong they are all wrong in the same direction.

## What you need in place

- **Accurate stock on hand**: With cycle counting to prove it, not an annual stocktake to correct it.
- **Contracted pricing as data**: So orders price themselves.
- **Receipting discipline**: Prompt and accurate, because payables and stock both depend on it.
- **Allocation policy**: Who gets constrained stock, decided in advance.
- **Supplier performance measurement**: On-time and in-full, measured rather than felt.
- **Landed cost**: Freight, duty and handling in the inventory value, or margin is overstated.

## Questions to ask

```qa
Stock accuracy :: What is our count accuracy, measured by cycle count? :: Everything downstream inherits this number.
Working capital :: How much cash is in stock, and how old is it? :: Ageing is where obsolescence hides.
Concentration :: Which items have a single supplier? :: Name them before you need to.
Receipting :: How quickly are receipts recorded? :: Lag here blocks invoices and misstates stock.
Landed cost :: Are freight and duty in the inventory value? :: If not, reported margin is wrong.
Allocation :: Who decides when there is not enough? :: Policy, or whoever escalates loudest.
```

## Metrics and KPIs to track

```metrics
Inventory accuracy :: Cycle count variance by value and by line.
Stock turns :: By category, against target.
Inventory ageing :: Value by age band, and obsolescence provision.
Supplier on-time in-full :: By supplier, against contract.
Fill rate :: Demand met from available stock.
Receipting lag :: Delivery to receipt recorded.
```
