## Where CRM sits in Lead-to-Cash

Lead-to-Cash is the stream that runs from a stranger's first interaction to money in the bank. CRM owns the front of it and hands off to supply chain and finance at the order. Treating it as a self-contained module is the most common way to end up with a sales system that nobody in finance trusts.

```steps
Contact to Lead :: Marketing generates interest and captures consent. A contact becomes a lead once there is enough signal to act on.
Lead to Opportunity :: The lead is qualified and handed to sales, who judge whether it is worth pursuing and convert it to an opportunity.
Opportunity to Quote :: Sales assess whether the opportunity is ready to be priced, and a quote is raised — by the rep, or by the customer through a web store.
Quote to Order :: The quote is negotiated until agreed, then converted into an order.
Order to Cash :: The order is fulfilled, invoiced and collected. This stage lives in supply chain and finance, not in CRM.
```

> The handover at Quote to Order is where most CRM implementations leak. If the order that lands in the ERP does not match the quote the customer agreed to, no amount of pipeline reporting will save you.

## What the module actually does

- **Contact management**: Centralises contact detail and the full history of interactions, so the relationship survives a change of account manager.
- **Sales management**: Tracks opportunities, pipeline and performance against target.
- **Marketing tools**: Campaign management, lead generation and the analysis that says which of them worked.
- **Customer support**: Service requests, ticketing and the feedback loop back into the record.
- **Analytics and reporting**: Insight into customer behaviour, sales trends and marketing effectiveness.

## Setting it up

- **Integration with other modules**: CRM must share data with sales, marketing and support, or you have simply bought a second customer database.
- **Data migration**: Existing customer data comes across checked for accuracy and completeness, not copied wholesale.
- **Customisation**: Tailor the system to the processes the business actually runs, rather than reshaping the business around a demo.
- **Security settings**: Access controls and permissions that reflect who should be able to see which customers.
- **User training**: Sales teams abandon CRM faster than any other module when it feels like admin. Training is adoption work, not a formality.

## Implementing it

```steps
Requirements gathering :: Define what this business needs CRM to do, in its own words.
Vendor selection :: Choose a CRM that integrates with the existing ERP, or an ERP whose built-in CRM is good enough.
Customisation and configuration :: Configure against the documented processes.
Data migration :: Move existing customer data with a cleansing pass, not a straight copy.
Integration :: Wire up real-time exchange with the other ERP modules.
Testing :: Prove the whole Lead-to-Cash path end to end, not each component in isolation.
Training :: Get users genuinely comfortable before go-live.
Go-live :: Launch, then watch adoption and data quality closely in the first weeks.
```

## Questions to ask vendors

```qa
Integration :: How does the CRM integrate with our existing ERP? :: Ask for the actual integration pattern and which objects sync in which direction, not a logo slide.
Customisation :: What customisation is available to fit our sales process? :: Establish what is configuration, what is code, and what will break on upgrade.
Privacy and security :: How does the CRM handle data privacy and security? :: Confirm where customer data is hosted and how access is controlled and audited.
Support :: What training and support services are offered? :: Pin down what is included in licence cost and what is billable.
Scale :: Can the CRM scale as the business grows? :: Test against realistic record volumes and user counts, not the current headcount.
```

## Metrics and KPIs to track

```metrics
Customer satisfaction score :: How customers rate their dealings with the business.
Sales conversion rate :: Share of qualified leads that become customers.
Average deal size :: Average revenue per closed deal.
Customer retention rate :: Share of customers retained over a period.
Marketing ROI :: Return generated by marketing spend.
```
