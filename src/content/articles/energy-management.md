## Why it matters

Energy sits in three places at once: an operating cost, an asset performance signal, and a disclosure obligation. Treating it only as a bill misses the other two.

- **It is a controllable cost**: Consumption follows how assets are operated, which is a decision rather than a given.
- **It is a condition signal**: A pump drawing more power for the same duty is telling you something before it fails.
- **It is reportable**: Emissions and energy disclosure requirements are tightening, and they need auditable data, not estimates.

## The workflow

```steps
Metering :: Consumption measured at a level where a decision can act on it, not just at the site boundary.
Baseline :: Normal consumption established against output, weather and operating pattern.
Monitoring :: Actual against baseline, with variance investigated rather than noted.
Analysis :: Consumption attributed to assets, processes and sites.
Action :: Operating changes, maintenance or capital investment prioritised on return.
Reporting :: Cost, consumption and emissions reported on the same data.
```

> Normalise before you compare. Consumption that rose ten percent while output rose fifteen is an improvement, and a report that shows only the rise will be argued with rather than acted on.

## What you need in place

- **Sub-metering where decisions are made**: Site-level data supports no action.
- **A normalised baseline**: Against production, occupancy or weather as appropriate.
- **Attribution to assets**: So energy joins maintenance and performance data.
- **Tariff awareness**: Peak demand charges often exceed consumption charges; shifting load is cheaper than reducing it.
- **An audit trail for reported figures**: Disclosure data needs the same rigour as financial data.

## Questions to ask

```qa
Granularity :: Can we attribute consumption to an asset or process? :: If only to a site, no operational decision follows.
Baseline :: Normalised against what? :: Raw comparison invites dispute and inaction.
Demand charges :: What share of cost is peak demand rather than consumption? :: Often the larger half, and the easier to change.
Reporting :: Would our disclosed figures survive audit? :: Estimated data is increasingly not acceptable.
Action :: What changed as a result of last year's data? :: Monitoring without action is a reporting exercise.
```

## Metrics and KPIs to track

```metrics
Energy intensity :: Consumption per unit of output or per square metre.
Variance to baseline :: Actual against normalised expectation.
Peak demand :: Against contracted capacity and charge thresholds.
Cost per unit :: Blended rate, trended against market.
Emissions :: Reported against target, on auditable data.
```
