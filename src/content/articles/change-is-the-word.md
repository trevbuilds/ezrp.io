## Why it matters

The system goes live on one day. The organisation changes over a year, and only if someone planned for it. Adoption is where the benefit is, and adoption is not a phase at the end.

- **Benefits come from behaviour**: A correctly configured process performed the old way delivers nothing.
- **Workarounds are permanent**: Whatever people do in the first month becomes the process.
- **It cannot be compressed**: Change is the thing that gets cut when the schedule slips, and it is the thing whose absence causes the failure.

## Putting it into practice

```steps
Impact assessment :: Role by role, what changes about how each person spends their day.
Sponsorship :: Visible, business-side ownership, held by someone whose result depends on it.
Engagement :: The people doing the work involved in the design, early enough to shape it.
Communication :: Honest about what changes and what is harder, not only what improves.
Capability :: Training on the process people will actually run, in the environment they will run it in.
Reinforcement :: Support, measurement and correction through the first full cycle, not the first week.
```

> Train on the process, not on the screens. People who know where the buttons are but not what the process expects will invent their own process, and it will be the old one.

## What you need in place

- **A change impact assessment by role**: Specific enough to be useful to a manager.
- **Business sponsorship with accountability for adoption**: Named, and measured on it.
- **Super users from the business**: Selected for credibility, given time, and kept after go-live.
- **Training in a realistic environment**: With the organisation's own data and scenarios.
- **Adoption measurement**: What people actually do in the system, monitored after go-live.
- **A workaround amnesty and hunt**: Find them in the first quarter and fix the cause.

## Questions to ask

```qa
Impact :: Whose job changes, and by how much? :: If we cannot answer role by role, change is not planned.
Sponsorship :: Who in the business is accountable for adoption? :: Not the programme — the business.
Capacity :: Do super users have time released for the role? :: A super user with a full day job is a title.
Training :: Will people train on real scenarios with real data? :: Generic training produces generic competence.
After :: Who supports the first month-end? :: The first close is where confidence is won or lost.
Measurement :: How will we know people actually adopted it? :: Usage data, not survey sentiment.
```

## Metrics and KPIs to track

```metrics
Change readiness by role :: Assessed before go-live, not assumed.
Training completion and competence :: Completed against assessed capable.
Adoption :: Transactions performed in the system as designed.
Workaround incidence :: Identified in the first quarter, and closed.
Support volume by process :: Where people are struggling, by area.
Benefit realisation :: Against baseline, by owner.
```
